A short local race: roll, leave base, avoid captures and bring both tokens home.

How to play Ludo Lite

  1. Two players share one screen, with two tokens each. Roll 6 to move a token from base to its starting cell. Otherwise move one active token by the rolled value.
  2. Each token travels 24 track steps to its protected home entry, then 4 private home steps, finishing at progress 28 on an exact roll. Landing on an opponent along the shared track sends that token to base, except at safe cells 1 and 13.
  3. A 6 keeps the turn; other rolls pass it after a move. If no move is legal, the turn resolves automatically. First player with both tokens home wins. This simplified variant omits several rules found in full Ludo.

Ludo Lite practice guide

A roll can have more than one useful destination

Moving the leading token is not automatically best. A second token may need to leave a vulnerable shared cell, while a token in the private home stretch is safe from capture. Read every legal option before choosing. Safe start cells protect tokens from capture, but they do not remove the need for an exact final roll.

One token needs two to finish

Your first token is at progress 26 and the second remains on the shared track. A roll of 4 cannot finish the first token because it would overshoot 28. Move the second token if its move is legal. A later roll of 2 can finish the first. Treat the private stretch as an exact-distance problem.

A focused practice session

  1. Read both highlighted token options before moving.
  2. Identify safe shared cells 1 and 13 on the track.
  3. Near the finish, calculate the exact roll each token needs.

What to keep in mind

Ludo Lite uses two tokens, a 24-cell shared track and four home steps per player. It is a deliberately smaller local variant, not a full tournament-rule implementation. Dice outcomes are not predictions of real-world luck.
