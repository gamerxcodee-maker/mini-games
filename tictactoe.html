How to play Tic Tac Toe

Players take turns placing X and O on a 3x3 grid. The first player to line up three of their own marks in a row, column, or diagonal wins the round. If all nine squares fill up with no line completed, the round is a draw.

2 Player, vs Computer, or Play with a Friend online

Switch to "2 Players" to pass the device back and forth with a friend, or pick "vs Computer" to play solo — the computer plays as O and always makes a valid, competitive move. Pick "Play with Friend" to connect with someone on a completely different device: one of you creates a room and shares the code or link, the other joins, and you play in real time.

How the online room works

The room connects your two browsers directly to each other using a technology called WebRTC — no account or sign-up needed. This works reliably on most home and mobile networks. If a connection doesn't go through on the first try, it's usually a restrictive network (like some office or school Wi-Fi) — refreshing and trying again, or switching to mobile data, usually fixes it.

Tips to get better at Tic Tac Toe

Taking a corner on your first move gives you more potential winning lines than taking an edge square. If your opponent takes the center, focus on blocking their obvious lines before building your own. Watch for "forks" — positions where your opponent could win two different ways on their next move — and shut them down early rather than reacting after the fact.

Frequently asked questions

Can Tic Tac Toe always end in a draw? Yes — with perfect play from both sides, every game ends in a draw. Beating the computer opponent takes a mistake on its part, since it plays to avoid losing.

Is there a way to never lose? Always take the center or a corner first, and always block your opponent's two-in-a-row before extending your own.

Does the online score reset between rounds? No — scores persist across rounds until you refresh the page or switch modes.
