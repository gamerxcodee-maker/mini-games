Typing practice: speed and accuracy together

Bright Arcade guide · Updated November 5, 2026

A typing score is useful when you understand what it measures. In Bright Arcade’s Typing Speed Test, WPM measures how much text you enter over time, while accuracy measures how closely your current text matches the passage. Use both numbers when comparing practice sessions.

Take a typing practice test

How this test starts and finishes

Choose a difficulty, click or tap the passage panel, and type the displayed passage. The timer starts with your first input. The test ends when you have entered as many characters as the passage contains, or when time runs out. Completing the length does not mean every character was correct; check the accuracy and error figures in the result.

A passage can finish before the limit. WPM uses the actual elapsed time, so a completed Easy passage is not necessarily a full one-minute test.

A worked WPM example

This test reports gross WPM: (characters entered ÷ 5) ÷ minutes elapsed, rounded to a whole number. The five-character unit includes spaces and punctuation; it is not a count of dictionary words.

If you enter 150 characters in 30 seconds, the calculation is (150 ÷ 5) ÷ 0.5 = 60 WPM. If 144 of those characters match the passage, accuracy is 144 ÷ 150 × 100 = 96%, with six mismatches remaining.

Gross WPM does not subtract an error penalty. A fast run with many mismatches can therefore have a high WPM and low accuracy. Looking only at your best WPM hides that difference.

What counts as an error here?

The test compares each character with the character at the same position in the passage. A missing letter can make later characters appear wrong until you correct the text. Case, spaces and punctuation matter. Backspace can correct your current text. The final Errors count measures mismatches still present at the end, not every mistake you made and later corrected.

A seven-session practice plan

  1. Record a baseline. Use Easy and note WPM, accuracy and whether you finished the passage.
  2. Slow down for accuracy. Practise familiar sentences without rushing past a mismatch.
  3. Notice repeated trouble. Look for a letter pair or punctuation mark that interrupts you.
  4. Practise that pattern. Type a few short examples slowly, then take another Easy test.
  5. Try Medium. Pay attention to capitals, commas and full stops.
  6. Repeat a comparable test. Use the same difficulty and keyboard so the result has useful context.
  7. Review both measures. Compare accuracy alongside WPM. More consistent, accurate runs are useful progress even when the highest score stays the same.

Keep sessions short enough to stay comfortable and take breaks when needed. This is a practice suggestion, not a promise of a particular speed increase.

Make comparisons fair

A phone keyboard, a physical keyboard and a different language or difficulty can produce different results. Avoid comparing them as identical tests. Passages are selected randomly, and repeating a familiar passage can also change your score. Best WPM is stored separately for each difficulty on this browser; it is not a verified leaderboard or certificate.
