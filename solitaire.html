How to play Solitaire

Build each foundation pile up from Ace to King, one suit at a time. In the tableau, stack cards in descending order with alternating colours (a red 9 on a black 10, for example). Tap the stock pile to draw a card to the waste pile, then tap a card to select it and tap the pile you want to move it to. Only Kings can start a new pile in an empty tableau column.

Tips for winning more games

Uncover the tableau's face-down cards as early as possible rather than rushing cards to the foundations, since a card sent to the foundation too early can sometimes block a useful tableau move later. Always move an Ace or a 2 to the foundation the moment it's available — there's rarely a reason to hold one back. When you have a choice between two equally valid moves, prefer the one that flips a new face-down card over one that doesn't.

Frequently asked questions

What happens when the stock runs out? Tap the empty stock pile to recycle the waste pile back into the stock, face down, ready to draw again.

Can I move a card back from the foundation? No — once a card is placed on a foundation in this version, it stays there.

When does Auto-Complete become available? When every tableau card is face up, the helper can move currently playable tableau and waste cards to foundations. It can stop if the remaining cards are blocked; you may still need to draw or rearrange cards.

Solitaire practice guide

Prefer a useful reveal over a move with no follow-up

Tableau columns build down in alternating colours, while each foundation builds up by suit from Ace to King. Tap a card or movable sequence, then its destination. Look for a move that exposes a face-down card and creates another option. Moving a card simply because it fits can leave the same hidden cards blocked. An empty tableau column is especially useful when a King or King-led sequence is available to occupy it.

A legal move should improve your options

A red 9 can be placed on an exposed black 10. If moving that 9 also reveals the face-down card beneath it, the move adds new information and may unlock another sequence. Putting the same 9 into a position that exposes nothing may still help, but you should be able to explain the follow-up. A red 9 cannot go on a red 10, even when their ranks look convenient.

A focused practice session

  1. Before drawing again, inspect whether a legal tableau move would reveal a hidden card.
  2. Track which suit needs the next foundation card rather than moving every available card immediately.
  3. When the stock is exhausted, recycle the waste and check whether newly opened tableau spaces make previously unusable cards useful.

What to keep in mind

This version cannot move cards back from foundations. Auto-Complete becomes available when tableau cards are face up, but it only transfers currently playable cards; it does not promise to solve every remaining stock or blocked sequence. Some deals may not lead to a win through your chosen decisions.
