Kakuro Mini

How to play Kakuro Mini

  1. Fill the nine white cells with digits 1–9. A number to the left is its row’s target sum; a number above is its column’s target sum.
  2. Digits cannot repeat within the same three-cell row or column. Reuse in a different row and different column is allowed.
  3. Press Check sums to validate the whole grid. Clear grid keeps the targets; New game creates another set. More than one valid completion may exist, and any completion meeting every rule wins.

Kakuro Mini practice guide

A sum is a set of possible combinations

For a three-cell run, list distinct-digit combinations that reach its target. Then compare them with crossing constraints. A digit can appear somewhere in a row without belonging in every square of that row. Keep the no-repeat rule separate from the arithmetic: reaching the total with repeated digits is still invalid.

A three-cell run totals six

Three distinct positive digits from 1 to 9 that total 6 must be 1, 2 and 3 in some order. The crossing column sums determine where each belongs. A row containing 2, 2 and 2 reaches the same total but breaks the distinct-digit rule, so it cannot be accepted.

A focused practice session

  1. Identify a low or high sum with few possible digit combinations.
  2. Use a crossing sum to decide one cell before completing its whole row.
  3. Check each run for repeated digits before pressing Check sums.

What to keep in mind

Kakuro Mini uses one 3×3 white block rather than a large grid of intersecting black-cell runs. Clues are generated from a valid arrangement, but uniqueness is not claimed.
